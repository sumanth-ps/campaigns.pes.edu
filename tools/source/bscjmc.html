Understanding journalism and mass communication

What is journalism and mass communication?

Journalism and mass communication is the study of how news, information and stories are gathered, verified, produced and delivered to large audiences — through print, television, radio, film, advertising, public relations and digital, social and AI-driven media. Today it combines field reporting and visual storytelling with data, technology and an understanding of how audiences think.

It is one of the most exciting fields a young person can choose right now, because it sits exactly where creativity, technology and society meet. Every brand needs storytellers, every organisation needs communicators, and every platform needs people who can create content that audiences genuinely care about. A journalism and mass communication degree prepares you for all of it.

New-age journalism and mass communication: field, data and AI

The media industry you will graduate into looks very different from the one most mass communication syllabi were written for, and that is wonderful news for students who are ready for it. Modern journalism and mass communication rests on three pillars, and the B.Sc. (Hons) at PES University is built around all three of them together.

01

It is field-oriented. Great stories still begin on the ground, with curiosity, conversation, observation and careful verification. Students of this course shoot, record and report in the field from their very first semester, using professional cameras, mobile journalism kits and audio equipment, and they learn early how rewarding it feels to bring back a story nobody else has told.

02

It is data-oriented. Newsrooms, agencies and brand teams now run on data: audience analytics, readership research, public datasets, market research and data visualisation. Students learn to find, clean, analyse and visualise data with confidence, and — just as importantly — to turn numbers into stories that ordinary people understand and remember.

03

It is AI-centric. Artificial intelligence now sits inside almost every stage of media work, from research, transcription and translation to scripting, editing, image and video generation, visual effects, audience insight and distribution. It also brings new challenges, such as deepfakes, synthetic images and AI-generated misinformation. Students of this course learn to use AI tools with genuine skill, and to detect and verify AI-manipulated content with equal confidence, so they leave as trusted voices in a noisy media world.

AI

AI across the journalism and mass communication curriculum

AI is not a single elective tucked away in a final semester; it runs right through the curriculum. Digital Photography and Imaging introduces AI imaging tools and the ethics of AI imagery in the very first semester. Digital Verification and OSINT trains students to trace sources and detect manipulated media like professional fact-checkers. Data Visualisation and Storytelling and Data and Investigative Journalism build strong analytical skills, while VFX and SFX with AI, AR and VR Technology for Media Professionals, and UI and UX Design Fundamentals prepare students for AI-assisted production, immersive media and digital product design

Praxis learning: you think, AI executes

Praxis simply means putting theory into practice, and it is the heartbeat of how this course is taught. Students own the thinking — the story idea, the angle, the research question, the creative direction, the ethical call and the critical judgement that makes work truly good. AI tools then help execute that vision at remarkable speed: transcribing interviews, drafting variations, analysing data, editing footage, generating visual concepts and adapting content for every platform.

Students review, verify, refine and sign off on every single output, so creative thinking and critical thinking always stay human while AI multiplies what one person can achieve. The result is a graduate who can confidently direct AI rather than compete with it — exactly the kind of media professional that newsrooms, content studios, agencies and corporate communication teams are excited to hire.

B.Sc. vs BA vs BJMC: which journalism and mass communication degree is better?

In India, journalism and mass communication is offered under several names — BJMC, BA JMC, BA Mass Communication and B.Sc. JMC — and it is completely natural to wonder which one is right for you. All of them can lead to rewarding media careers. The real difference lies in orientation: what each degree emphasises, how students are assessed, and what graduates can confidently do on their first day at work.

PES University's B.Sc. (Hons) takes a practical, quantitative, data-centric and technology-led approach to journalism and mass communication. BA programmes typically take a more theory-centric approach, with greater weight on media history, communication theory and humanities electives. Both develop writing and critical thinking, which remain at the heart of this course too; the B.Sc. simply adds the data, technical, business and production skills that the modern media industry increasingly expects.

Experiential learning and campus life at PES

Experiential learning: hands-on journalism and mass communication training

Most of what students of this course learn, they learn by doing — and that is what makes every week so energising. Industry-oriented, practical training runs through every semester: in the studio, in the field, in the newsroom and face to face with working professionals. Here is what hands-on journalism and mass communication education looks like, week after week.

Every Thursday: learn directly from the industry

Every Thursday, a professional from the media and communication industry comes to campus to teach — journalists, filmmakers, photographers, content creators, advertising and PR professionals, entrepreneurs and communication leaders. They share what they know, the lessons they learned the hard way and how they built their careers. Students get to question them, interact with them and form connections that help build strong resumes and portfolios, and many leave each session genuinely inspired.

Recent guests have included senior journalist Uday Kumar of Prajavani; Priti David, Executive Director of the People's Archive of Rural India (PARI), on writing for social change; filmmaker, choreographer and writer Yogesh Master; Rahul Raj Reddy, founder of Surge Production House, on media entrepreneurship and content creation; T. Studio practice: photography, videography and visual storytelling

Visual and content-centric subjects carry real weight in this course. Students train in digital photography and imaging, videography and visual storytelling, cinematography, anchoring, broadcast production and podcasting, building skills that are in demand across every corner of the media industry.

On location, students take cameras, audio kits and lights out into Bengaluru to shoot short films, documentary sequences, news packages and photo essays. In the studio, they record news bulletins, interviews and panel discussions, taking turns as anchor, producer, camera operator and editor, so every student experiences every role.

Product and brand photography: shooting for real brands

Commercial photography is one of the most practical skills a media graduate can carry into the workplace, and students of this course practise it the professional way. In studio product photography sessions — including one run in collaboration with Aura Crafting — students plan the shot with faculty guidance, design the lighting, direct the scene, and capture and edit images ready for real commercial use.

Brand shoot simulations take this further. Working from creative briefs, students produce advertising-style photographs of everyday consumer products, handling concept, lighting, product placement and post-production to publication standard. Every shoot ends with portfolio-ready images and a genuine understanding of how advertising and brand communication work in the real world.

Workshops, simulations and field visits

Beyond the studio, students build confidence through hands-on workshops led by industry experts, newsroom and production simulations, faculty-guided research projects and professional interview assignments. Field visits take students inside newsrooms, production houses and media organisations to see how the industry works day to day. In one memorable workshop on communication through mime, students learned to tell stories without a single word — sharpening body language, visual thinking and the kind of expressive confidence that serves them in front of and behind the camera. Students have also spent time at Asianet Suvarna News, experiencing a working news channel first-hand and reporting on camera alongside the channel's team.

Publications and corporate communication

Students write, edit, design and produce publications — newspapers, magazines, newsletters, reports, feature packages and brand publications. There is something deeply satisfying about holding a finished publication you created with your own team, and the process sharpens precise writing, editing, layout and editorial judgement.

These skills transfer directly into corporate communication, where in-house magazines, newsletters, annual reports, press releases and brand content are central to the role. Students who master publications leave with a clear advantage for careers in corporate communication, public relations and brand teams. Students of this course have already seen their work in print in Voices of Media.

The newsroom exercise: verify, correct, rewrite

Accuracy is taught, not assumed. In the newsroom exercise, each student receives a long, unedited news copy seeded with factual errors that are deliberately hard to spot. They verify every claim, log their sources, correct what is wrong and rewrite the story tightly to length — the same discipline a professional copy desk applies on deadline, and a skill that earns trust for a lifetime.

Launching a publication, from data to registration

Students work through what it truly takes to start a newspaper in India. They analyse a large readership dataset spanning several Indian cities, define an audience from the numbers, build an editorial identity, plan sources, printing and distribution, and understand registration with the Press Registrar General of India. It is quantitative, practical and completely real-world, and it gives students the confidence to imagine launching something of their own.

Real industry cases: real organisations, real problems

Students work on real industry cases built around real organisations and the actual problems they faced — in journalism, broadcasting, advertising, public relations and corporate communication. They analyse each situation, propose and defend their solutions, and earn their marks through the quality of their thinking. Coursework becomes rehearsal for the decisions they will make at work, and students discover how capable they already are.

Campus media: covering the university every week

Students of this course form the media coordination teams for weekly events across ten departments of PES University. Each team plans coverage, shoots stills and horizontal and vertical video, and delivers edited media and reports — every week of term. Senior and junior students work together in mixed crews, so skills and confidence pass naturally from one batch to the next. It is the closest thing to a working newsroom a campus can offer, and it runs all year round.

Journalism and mass communication is the study of how news, information and stories are gathered, verified, produced and delivered to large audiences through print, television, radio, film, advertising, public relations and digital, social and AI-driven media. Today it combines field reporting with data, technology and audience understanding.

It leads to a wide range of careers — journalism, film and video, photography, advertising, public relations, digital and social media, UX design, data and verification work, and media entrepreneurship. The broader the skills a programme teaches, the wider the options; this programme covers reporting, production, brand communication, technology and research.

It covers the same field as a BJMC or BA JMC — journalism, broadcast, film, advertising, public relations and digital media — and adds a technical and analytical strand including data journalism, UI/UX design, media research, sound and cinematography. It is a four-year honours degree under the National Education Policy, with an exit option after three years.
